Check the evidence before you share it.

MedCheck shows how strong the science is behind the health claims you see on TikTok, Instagram and group chats. Every rating explains what was studied, what's still unknown, and links to the real sources.

How it works

Search a claim the way you'd say it out loud. MedCheck finds the closest checked claim.

1. Search

Type something like "does wet hair give you a cold" or browse the claims going around right now.

2. See the rating

A four-level meter shows how strong the evidence is, with a one-line answer in plain language.

3. Read the sources

Each claim links to the studies and reviews it's based on, plus what would change the rating.

4. Save and share

Save claims to come back to, and share a link so friends see the evidence too.

What a check looks like

We don't say "true" or "false." Health science is messier than that, so we rate how strong the evidence is and say why.

See this claim in the app
Limited evidence

Vitamin C prevents the common cold

It doesn't prevent colds for most people, but may modestly shorten them.

Source: Cochrane systematic review

The four ratings

Every claim gets one of these, based on the kind and amount of research behind it.

Strong evidence

A systematic review of randomized trials, or several large trials, found consistent results that support the claim.

Limited evidence

Some good evidence, but the effect is small, applies only to some people, or comes mostly from observational studies.

Unclear / mixed

Few studies, conflicting results, or low-quality studies, so we can't say either way yet.

Contradicts claim

Good-quality studies show the claim is false.

About MedCheck

MedCheck was started by high school students and won first place in our district in the Congressional App Challenge. We built it because health misinformation spreads fast, and checking it usually means reading studies most people never see.

We start from strong sources like Cochrane reviews, the CDC, the WHO and major medical journals, and we check every rating against its sources by hand before it's published. MedCheck is for learning, not medical advice. For your own health, talk to a doctor, nurse or pharmacist.
